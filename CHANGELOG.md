#### 2.3.0

> 3 September 2026

Additive. Broadcasting arrives as a resource, subscriptions gain the three lifecycle operations that were readable but not writable, and the audience a broadcast addresses becomes something you can actually describe.

##### Added — Broadcast resource

A broadcast could be started from the Subscriby dashboard and from the Telegram bot, and from no automation surface at all. Three operations close that:

- **Send** — queue a Telegram message to a segment of a project's members.
- **Preview** — size an audience without sending anything.
- **List Audiences** — every segment with its current recipient count.

**Preview before you Send.** A broadcast cannot be recalled, every recipient is a real person, and the send is queued — so the Send response describes what was *addressed*, not what was *delivered*. Watch **Broadcast — Completed** for the sent and failed tallies.

Recipient counts are smaller than the member counts elsewhere in the node, and that is correct rather than a bug: a broadcast travels through the project's connected bot, so it reaches only members who have opened a chat with it. Anyone who subscribed through a portal page has never messaged the bot.

##### Added — Audience targeting beyond member status

The original five segments answered a member's *status*, which cannot express most of what a creator wants to say. Four segments and two fields fix that.

Four new segments, each a fact about a subscription rather than about the member:

- **Expiring Soon** — access runs out within the horizon you set.
- **Cancelled, Still Inside Their Period** — they turned renewal off but have not gone yet. Still reachable, still winnable.
- **Paused Subscriptions** — paused rather than ended.
- **Trialing Without a Card** — on trial with no card on file, so they will not convert on their own.

Two fields that narrow whichever segment you picked:

- **Plan ID** composes with the segment rather than replacing it. *Customers* plus a plan reaches people paying for it right now; *Churned* plus a plan reaches people who held it and left. The plan and the state always describe the **same** subscription, so someone paying for Silver who once tried Gold is not a Gold customer. Rejected for *Leads*, who never subscribed, and for the pass segments, whose plan is implied by the window.
- **Running Out Within (Days)** sets the Expiring Soon horizon, 1–90, default 7.

One behaviour worth knowing before you build a renewal nudge: **an auto-renewing subscription is never Expiring Soon.** A subscription's end date is rewritten to the new period end on every renewal, so a date alone describes the next *invoice*, not an expiry — counting it would sweep every monthly subscriber into the segment once a month. Someone appears here only once their access genuinely lapses: they turned renewal off, or the plan does not renew at all.

##### Added — Subscription lifecycle operations

- **Pause Access** — suspend a member's resource access. Billing is unaffected and continues.
- **Unpause Access** — restore suspended access and issue fresh invite links. The old links were revoked at pause and do not come back.
- **Reactivate** — call off a scheduled cancellation. Stripe only; other providers error.

##### Added — 2 trigger events

Bringing the catalogue to **106**.

- **Broadcast — Queued**, when a send starts, carrying the audience it resolved to and how many members it will reach.
- **Broadcast — Completed**, carrying the `sent` and `failed` tallies.

Both payloads now also carry `plan_id` and `expiring_within_days`, so a workflow can tell two broadcasts to the same segment apart.

Both fire for broadcasts started anywhere — the dashboard, the Telegram bot, the API, or this node.

#### 2.1.0

> 31 August 2026

Additive. Teams, roles and groups become writable, and two new trigger events cover the transitions that had no signal.

##### Added — Team, Team Member, Role and Group writes

Each of these resources could already be read, and their events could already be listened for. You could be told a role changed and not change one. Nine operations close that:

- **Team** — Create, Update, Delete.
- **Team Member** — Invite, Update Role, Remove, Cancel Invitation.
- **Role** — Create, Update, Delete.
- **Group** — Create, Update, Delete, Sync Members.

Two behaviours to know before wiring them up:

- **Permissions replace, they do not merge.** Sending the permissions field on Role → Update or Group → Update makes that list the entire set. Omit it to leave the existing permissions alone.
- **Group → Sync Members is a sync, not an add.** Anyone missing from the list is removed from the group, and an empty list empties it. Read the group first if you mean to append.

**Invite** is addressed by email rather than user ID, because the invitee may not have a Subscriby account yet — that is what separates inviting from adding. Its role field takes a role **code** that must already exist on the team, so create the role first.

Creating and re-permissioning need the Growth plan. Deleting and removing do not: a creator whose plan lapsed still has collaborators attached, and has to be able to take access away.

##### Added — 2 trigger events

- **Team — Updated**, when a team is renamed. `name` is the only mutable field a team has.
- **Group — Members Synced**, when the people in a group change. It carries `added_ids` and `removed_ids` alongside the final list, so a workflow mirroring access control does not have to diff two snapshots to see who moved.

**Team Member → Update Role also makes an existing trigger reachable.** `team.member.role_changed` has been in the catalogue and offered by this node for several releases, but nothing in Subscriby could raise it until the API gained this operation.

##### Catalogue

104 events — full parity with Subscriby's `WebhookEvent` enum.
#### 2.0.0

> 31 August 2026

**Breaking.** The Plan resource's Create and Update operations now take a **Plan Kind** and the fields belonging to it. Everything else is additive.

##### Plan writes are kind-scoped

The Subscriby API moved plans to a discriminated union: `kind` names exactly one nested object — `billing`, `pass` or `pass_series` — and a block belonging to another kind is now refused rather than ignored. The node mirrors that with a **Plan Kind** selector whose choice drives which fields appear.

**Plan → Create did not work before this release.** It sent `currency` where the API expects `currency_id`, a `billing_cycle` value (`monthly` / `yearly` / `one_time`) that is not in the API's enum, and never sent the required `resources` — so every call returned 422. Nobody could have had a working Plan → Create workflow, which is why the field changes here carry no migration burden.

##### Added — Pass Series

A **Pass Series** sells a curated slate of *other* pass plans' dated windows for one payment.

- **Pass Window → List** (new resource) — the dated windows a project's pass plans generate, with their IDs, local times, status, on-sale state and holder counts. **A series points at windows that already exist**, so this is where its window IDs come from; without it a series could not be authored from n8n at all. Filters by plan, status, on-sale-only and date range.
- Plan → Create with kind **Pass Series** takes those IDs, a seat limit, a sales cutoff measured against the whole season, a next-season link with a holders-only presale, and **Automatic Inclusion Rules** as a repeating collection.
- A rule describes windows rather than naming them and keeps matching after the save: a window scheduled later is added to the slate **and granted to everyone already holding the series**, at no charge. Handpicked IDs never grow on their own. The two compose.

##### Added — 11 trigger events

Eight `pass_series.*` — Purchased, Pass Completed, Completed, Pass Added, Pass Substituted, Pass Dropped, Seats Exhausted, Presale Opened.

Three `pass.*` that had no event at all before: **Window Scheduled** (a window coming into existence — previously a schedule could not be mirrored outward without polling), **Holder Queued** (a buyer tapped their invite link and is waiting), and **Holder Missed** (a window closed and one holder never turned up; Window Closed only reported an aggregate count).

Two payload facts worth knowing: `pass_series.leg_dropped` carries `refund_per_holder`, which is **a figure, not an action** — Subscriby never moves the money, so treating it as "already refunded" will double-refund. And `pass_series.purchased` fires **in addition to** `subscription.activated`, because a series is a subscription; de-duplicate on `subscription_id` if you use both.

##### Changed

- Plan → Create's **Price** is now a decimal rather than "the smallest currency unit". It always was decimal on the API side; the field description was wrong.
- The README's events table is now generated from `nodes/SubscribyTrigger/events.ts` by `npm run sync:readme`. It had drifted to listing two events in the `pass` family when there were eight.

##### Catalogue

102 events — full parity with Subscriby's `WebhookEvent` enum.

#### 1.4.0

> 27 August 2026

Adds the `billing.trial_ending` event to the trigger node.

- **Billing — Trial Ending** (`billing.trial_ending`) fires while the creator's own Subscriby trial is still running, before it converts and takes its first payment. Previously the trial converted silently and the earliest observable signal was `billing.invoice_created`, after the charge.

  It fires **once per reminder milestone** rather than once per trial — 72 hours out and again 24 hours out on the default ladder. Branch on `hours_remaining`, or de-duplicate on `subscription_id`, if the workflow should only run once.

  `amount` is a decimal string paired with an uppercase ISO `currency`. When `amount_estimated` is `true` the figure is the plan's list price because the upcoming invoice was unreachable — do not reconcile against it.

#### 1.3.0

> 21 August 2026

Adds quote-reply to the Support Conversation resource.

- **Support Conversation → Reply** takes an optional **Quote Message ID**. Setting it renders the quoted message above your reply, the way a chat client shows a threaded reply, instead of the recipient having to guess which question is being answered.

  The id must name a message in the *same* conversation. Anything else is refused by the API rather than dropped, so a quote can never surface one member's words in another member's thread.

- Renamed the reply mode label from **Internal Note** to **Private Note**, matching the Subscriby dashboard.

#### 1.2.0

> 20 August 2026

Adds the coupon and support event families, the Support Conversation resource on the action node, and brings the catalogue back to parity with the upstream event enum.

- Added the seven `coupon.*` events — `created`, `updated`, `activated`, `deactivated`, `deleted`, `redeemed`, `exhausted` — covering the coupon subsystem Subscriby ships alongside this release.
- Added `project.bot.status_changed`. The catalogue had been one event behind `WebhookEvent` since that case was introduced upstream, so a workflow could not react to a bot becoming unreachable without polling `GET /v1/projects/{project}/bot` on a schedule.
- Added the six `support.*` events — `conversation.opened`, `conversation.assigned`, `conversation.resolved`, `conversation.reopened`, `message.received`, `message.sent` — for Subscriby's member support inbox. A member who sends a project bot something it does not recognise now opens a support conversation, and these events route that traffic into whatever helpdesk, ops channel or on-call rota the workflow points at.

  `support.message.received` deliberately omits the message body. Fetch it with **Support Conversation → List Messages** so member text never lands in webhook delivery logs. `support.message.sent` does not fire for internal notes, which stay private to the creator's team.

- Added the **Support Conversation** resource to the action node, the twentieth: List, Get, List Messages, Reply, Resolve and Assign. A Reply reaches the member on Telegram attributed to the creator rather than the bot, so an automated response is not mistaken for one the creator wrote by hand. Setting **Internal Note** records a private remark for the team instead of messaging the member, and Assign with an empty assignee clears an existing assignment rather than erroring.
- `EVENT_CATALOG` now matches `WebhookEvent` exactly at 87 events across 14 families.
- The package passed n8n's Creator Portal review and is now listed as a **verified community node**, so it installs without the community-node warning.

#### 1.1.0

> 17 August 2026

Adds the time-limited pass events, taking the catalogue from 71 to 73.

- Added `pass.window_opened` and `pass.window_closed`. A time-limited pass sells access to a scheduled window rather than an open-ended membership, and these are the two moments a workflow can hang off: the window opening, when buyers are admitted, and it closing, when they are removed. Without them the only signal a pass produced was the original payment, hours or days earlier.
- Corrected the retirement date quoted in the `1.0.0` entry below, which had been wrong twice in opposite directions.

#### 1.0.4

> 11 August 2026

Fixes the regression in `1.0.3`. **Do not submit `1.0.3` for verification — it fails the scan.**

- Removed the `overrides` field. `@n8n/community-nodes/no-overrides-field` rejects it outright: community packages install into an isolated dependency tree, so overrides achieve nothing and are treated as boilerplate. `1.0.3` traded one scanner error for another.
- Pinned `@n8n/node-cli` to `0.44.0` instead. That is the first release whose bundled `@n8n/eslint-plugin-community-nodes` is `0.29.0` — the same version `@n8n/scan-community-package@beta` uses, and the version where `node-usable-as-tool` became trigger-aware. Aligning the toolchain gets the correct rule without a package.json override or an `eslint.config.mjs` edit, which `"strict": true` forbids anyway.

The version is exact rather than a range on purpose: `0.43.2`, still tagged `latest`, bundles plugin `0.28.0` and would silently reintroduce the contradiction.

#### 1.0.0

> 10 August 2026

First release under the Subscriby name. This package replaces `n8n-nodes-memberpass`, which is deprecated on npm. It keeps working through the legacy API bridge until 14 August 2027, when `memberpass.net` is decommissioned — migrate well before then.

The npm package name is new, so the version number restarts at 1.0.0. Functionally this is a superset of `n8n-nodes-memberpass@1.4.0` — nothing was removed.

Every identifier changed, and n8n does not migrate across package names. Existing workflows must be rebuilt rather than upgraded in place:

| | Old | New |
| --- | --- | --- |
| npm package | `n8n-nodes-memberpass` | `n8n-nodes-subscriby` |
| Action node | `n8n-nodes-memberpass.memberPass` | `n8n-nodes-subscriby.subscriby` |
| Trigger node | `n8n-nodes-memberpass.memberPassTrigger` | `n8n-nodes-subscriby.subscribyTrigger` |
| Credential | `memberPassApi` | `subscribyApi` |
| Trigger webhook path | `memberpass` | `subscriby` |

Because the webhook path changed, every trigger's production URL changes and the matching webhook endpoint must be re-registered on the Subscriby side. See the [migration guide](https://docs.subscriby.net/guide/reference/rebrand-migration).

- Renamed the package, both nodes, the credential and the trigger webhook path from MemberPass to Subscriby.
- Repointed the default API base URL to `https://api.subscriby.net/v1`, and the credential now expects an `sbt_`-prefixed token.
- Repointed all documentation links to `docs.subscriby.net`.
- Corrected the codex node identifiers to the `n8n-nodes-subscriby.` prefix, which had incorrectly used the `n8n-nodes-base.` prefix reserved for built-in nodes.
- Added the MIT `LICENSE` file, a `bugs` URL, and a `clean` step to the build.
- Carried forward pagination support for list operations, added in `n8n-nodes-memberpass@1.4.0`.
